Phase 4

No new sounds. Children read and spell words with adjacent consonants (blends) using the sounds already known.

Reception writing rules

How to choose the right spelling when writing.

Hold the pencil and form letters correctly

Start each letter in the right place and finish it in the right direction. Lower case for most writing; capital letter for names.

Sama b c

Segment to spell

Say the word, count the sounds on your fingers, then write one grapheme for each sound.

c-a-t → catsh-o-p → shop

ff, ll, ss, zz and ck after a short vowel

At the end of a short word, double f, l, s, z, and use ck, straight after a short vowel.

offbellkissbuzzduck

Tricky words

Some common words have tricky parts that don't follow the code. Learn them by sight.

thetoInogointoheshewemebewasmyyoutheyherallare

Writing a simple sentence

Capital letter at the start, finger spaces between words, full stop at the end.

I can see a red bus.

Year 1 writing rules

How to choose the right spelling when writing.

ff, ll, ss, zz, ck

Doubled after a single short vowel at the end of short words. Exceptions: if, pal, us, bus, yes.

offwellmissbuzzback

The /ng/ sound before k

Write n, not ng, before k.

bankthinkhonksunk

Dividing words into syllables

Each syllable has a vowel sound. Clap it, then spell one syllable at a time.

pocketrabbitcarrotthundersunset

tch after a short vowel

The /ch/ sound after a single short vowel is usually tch. Exceptions: rich, which, much, such.

catchfetchkitchennotchhutch

v at the end of words

English words hardly ever end in v, so add e.

havelivegive

Plurals: -s and -es

Add s for most plurals. If the ending sounds like an extra syllable (after s, x, z, sh, ch), add es.

catsdogsspendsrockscatchesboxeswishes

-ing, -ed, -er where the root doesn't change

Just add the ending. -ed can sound like /id/, /t/ or /d/ but is always spelt ed.

huntinghuntedhunterbuzzingjumpedplayed

-er and -est on adjectives

Add straight on when the root word doesn't change.

grandergrandestfresherfreshestquickerquickest

ay at the end, ai in the middle

The long /ai/ sound is ai inside a word and ay at the end.

rainwaitdayplay

oy at the end, oi in the middle

Same pattern as ai/ay.

coinboiltoyenjoy

ow at the end, ou in the middle

For the /ow/ sound in cow. ow is also used before n and l.

outloudcownowdownowl

/oa/: oa in the middle, ow or oe at the end

oa is rare at the end. o_e is also common in the middle.

boatroadsnowgrowtoegoeshome

/oo/: oo or u_e in the middle, ue or ew at the end

moontunebluecluechewgrew

/ee/ at the end: y in longer words

At the end of a word with two or more syllables, /ee/ is usually y. Short words use ee or ea.

happyfunnyverypartyseetea

/k/ at the end: ck or k

ck straight after a short vowel. k after a long vowel or a consonant.

backduckbookseekmilkbank

/or/ at the end: ore or aw

Not or at the end of most words.

morescoresawdrawfor

k before e, i, y

The /k/ sound is usually c, but k before e, i and y.

Kentsketchkitskinfrisky

Prefix un-

Adding un- changes the meaning to 'not'. The root word stays the same.

unhappyundounloadunfairunlock

Compound words

Two whole words joined together keep their normal spelling.

footballplaygroundfarmyardbedroomblackberry

Days of the week

Always start with a capital letter.

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFridaySaturdaySunday

Year 2 writing rules

How to choose the right spelling when writing.

/j/ at the end: dge or ge

After a short vowel use dge. Anywhere else use ge.

badgeedgebridgeagehugechangecharge

Soft c before e, i, y

c says /s/ before e, i and y.

raceicecellcityfancy

Silent letters: kn, gn, wr

The k, g and w are written but not said.

knockknowgnatsignwritewrongwrap

/ul/ at the end: le, el, al, il

le is most common. el after m, n, r, v, w and s. al and il in fewer words.

tableapplecameltunnelmetalpencil

y for /igh/ at the end

Use y for /igh/ at the end of short words.

cryflydrytryreplyJuly

ie before a final s or d

When a word ends in ie, or ends in y and gets -s/-es or -ed, you write ie before the s or d. It says /igh/ in short words and /ee/ in longer ones.

tie → ties, tiedlie → liescry → cries, criedfly → fliesbaby → babiescarry → carried

Changing y to i: -es, -ed, -er, -est

When a word ends in consonant + y, change y to i before adding es, ed, er or est.

fliestriesrepliescopiedhappierhappiest

Keep the y before -ing

Don't change y before -ing, or you would get ii.

copyingcryingreplying

Drop the e before -ing, -ed, -er, -est, -y

If the root ends in e, drop it before a vowel ending. Exception: being.

hikinghikedhikernicernicestshiny

Double the consonant

Single short vowel + single consonant: double the consonant before -ing, -ed, -er, -est, -y.

pattingpattedhummingsadderfattestrunny

/or/ written a before l and ll

allballcallwalktalkalways

/u/ written o

othermotherbrothernothingMonday

After w: /ur/ is or, /or/ is ar

After w, /ur/ is usually or and /or/ is usually ar.

wordworkwormworldwarwarmtowards

/o/ after w and qu is a

wantwatchwanderquantitysquash

Suffixes -ment, -ness, -ful, -less, -ly

Add straight on if the root ends in a consonant. Consonant + y changes y to i.

enjoymentsadnesscarefulhopelessbadlymerrimenthappinessplentiful

Years 3–4 writing rules

How to choose the right spelling when writing.

Prefixes un-, dis-, mis-

Add straight on. The root never changes, so dis + satisfied keeps both s's.

disappointdisagreedissatisfiedmisbehavemisleadunkind

Prefix in- and its forms il-, im-, ir-

in- means 'not'. Before l it becomes il, before m or p im, before r ir.

inactiveincorrectillegalillegibleimmatureimpossibleirregularirrelevant

Prefixes re-, sub-, inter-, super-, anti-, auto-

re = again, sub = under, inter = between, super = above, anti = against, auto = self.

redosubwayinternationalsupermarketantisepticautograph

Doubling in longer words

If the last syllable is stressed and ends in one vowel + one consonant, double the consonant before a vowel suffix. If it isn't stressed, don't double.

forgettingforgottenbeginningpreferredgardeninglimitedlimitation

Suffix -ation

Added to verbs to make nouns. Drop a final e first.

informationadorationsensationpreparationadmiration

Suffix -ly and its changes

Usually add straight on. y after a consonant becomes i. -le becomes -ly. -ic becomes -ically. Exceptions: truly, duly, wholly, publicly.

sadlyhappilyangrilygentlysimplyhumblybasicallyfrantically

Endings -ture and -sure

/cher/ is usually ture. /zher/ is sure.

picturenatureadventuremeasuretreasureenclosure

Choosing -tion, -ssion, -sion or -cian

Look at the root word. t or te → tion. ss or mit → ssion. d or se → sion. c or cs → cian (usually a person).

inventionactionexpressionpermissiontensionexpansionmusicianelectricianmagician

Suffix -ous

Keep e after soft g (courageous). our becomes or (humorous). y becomes i (various). Some roots aren't words on their own (enormous).

famouspoisonousdangerouscourageoushumorousvariousseriousobviouscuriousenormousjealous

Possessive apostrophe with plurals

Plural ending in s: apostrophe after the s. Irregular plural: add 's.

girls'boys'babies'children'smen'smice's
